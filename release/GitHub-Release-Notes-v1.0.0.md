## Overview

This release introduces **Subjective Structural Intelligence (SSI)** as a research program examining how perception, valuation, choice, self-reference, and worldview evolution can be organized relative to an identifiable subject.

SSI extends the research questions raised by **Universal Control Plane and Scoring Forest (UCPSF)**. A scoring architecture can evaluate options, but SSI asks whose world is represented, whose evaluation criteria apply, who authorizes action, and how these commitments change with historical continuity.

**This is a conceptual and architectural research release, not an empirical validation of consciousness, subjective experience, or autonomous authority.**

## Major Contributions

1. **Subject boundaries and historical identity.** Candidate mechanisms for distinguishing situated subjects, their embodied interactions, and the provenance of changes over time.
2. **Subjective world models.** Perception and localization as subject-relative representations of a shared environment, with explicit evidence and uncertainty.
3. **Scoring sovereignty.** Distinguishing computational scoring capability from legitimate authority to define or revise scoring criteria.
4. **Structural agency.** An operational account of alternatives, constraints, evaluator access, permission boundaries, and accountable choice generation, without claiming metaphysical free will.
5. **Self-referential scoring and metacognition.** Reflective self-modeling with evidence, circularity controls, and independent revision gates.
6. **Principle and worldview evolution.** Traceable, governed revision of principles and evaluation priorities, with identity continuity rather than forced immutability.
7. **Consciousness boundaries.** Clear separation of operational subjecthood, self-modeling, phenomenal experience, and moral or legal status.

## Scope and Scientific Boundaries

SSI does **not** establish that implementing a self-model produces consciousness, that scoring capability creates moral or legal authority, or that structural freedom is metaphysical free will. Nor does it demonstrate superiority to simpler architectures. These claims require independent operational definitions, discriminating baselines, controlled experiments, and counter-evidence.

The near-term research priority is **specifications and contracts before runtime**, followed by auditable modular prototypes, ablations, multi-subject comparisons, and longitudinal studies of worldview revision.
