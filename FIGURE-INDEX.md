## 2. Individual figure specifications and placement

### Fig-001 — SSI Grand Map

**Planned file:** `figures/Fig-001-SSI-Grand-Map.png`  
**Primary placement:** `docs/SSI-001-From-Structural-Intelligence-to-Subjective-Structural-Intelligence.md`, immediately after **Section 5, “A Subject-Centered Decision and Learning Loop”** (or after its introductory explanation).  
**Secondary placement:** repository `README.md`, immediately below the opening research thesis.

**Caption:** *Figure 1. SSI Grand Map. An identifiable subject organizes observations through boundary- and history-conditioned perception, subject-relative valuation, accountable choice, self-modeling, and principle/worldview revision; feedback supports subsequent structural growth.*

**Design checks:** Display the feedback loop, distinguish external reality from the Subjective World Model (SWM), and avoid depicting a single score as the subject itself. This is an architectural proposal, not a validated cognitive model.

### Fig-002 — From UCPSF to SSI

**Planned file:** `figures/Fig-002-From-UCPSF-to-SSI.png`  
**Primary placement:** `docs/SSI-001-From-Structural-Intelligence-to-Subjective-Structural-Intelligence.md`, in **Section 6, “SSI and UCPSF: Two Distinct Research Programs”**, after the scope comparison.  
**Secondary placement:** `README.md`, in the UCPSF–SSI relationship discussion.

**Caption:** *Figure 2. From UCPSF to SSI. UCPSF investigates scoring, evaluator composition, folding, and control; SSI asks whose world is represented, whose values govern scoring, who may choose, and how the subject evolves. The programs have bidirectional interfaces but remain independently scoped.*

**Design checks:** Avoid implying that SSI is a renamed UCPSF module or that UCPSF alone determines evaluative legitimacy.

### Fig-003 — Subject Boundary and Embodied Intelligence

**Planned file:** `figures/Fig-003-Subject-Boundary-and-Embodied-Intelligence.png`  
**Primary placement:** `docs/SSI-002-Subject-Boundary-Embodiment-and-Historical-Identity.md`, after the section introducing the integrated **Subject–Boundary–Embodiment–History architecture**.  
**Secondary placement:** `docs/SSI-003-Perception-Localization-and-the-Subjective-World-Model.md`, in **Section 2, “Perception Is Not Raw Input”** or **Section 7, “The Subject-World Coupling Loop.”**

**Caption:** *Figure 3. Subject Boundary and Embodied Intelligence. Information, resource, action, and governance boundaries mediate situated perception and action; embodied or operational capabilities shape affordances; historical transformations contribute to operational continuity.*

**Design checks:** Boundary is not subjecthood; embodiment is not necessarily biological; authorization is not capability; history is not identical to current state.

### Fig-004 — Scoring Forest and Worldview Architecture

**Planned file:** `figures/Fig-004-Scoring-Forest-and-Worldview-Architecture.png`  
**Primary placement:** `docs/SSI-004-Scoring-Forests-Valuation-and-Worldview-Formation.md`, after **Section 7, “From Valuation to Worldview Formation”**, or near **Section 8, “The Worldview Formation and Revision Loop (WFRL).”**  
**Secondary placement:** `docs/SSI-003-Perception-Localization-and-the-Subjective-World-Model.md`, near **Section 10, “Scoring Forests and Perception Localization.”**

**Caption:** *Figure 4. Scoring Forest and Worldview Architecture. Evidence and subject-relative interpretations feed context-sensitive evaluators; evaluation provenance, counter-evidence, and authorization constrain changes in valuation and worldview organization.*

**Design checks:** Distinguish score calculation from value legitimacy and separate evaluator updates from authorized deployment. A worldview is not merely a weighted score vector.

### Fig-005 — Structural Freedom and Choice Generation

**Planned file:** `figures/Fig-005-Structural-Freedom-and-Choice-Generation.png`  
**Primary placement:** `docs/SSI-005-Structural-Agency-Choice-and-Functional-Freedom.md`, after **Section 5, “From Scoring Forest to Choice”**, or adjacent to **Section 4, “Functional Freedom Is Not Option Count.”**  
**Secondary placement:** `cases/CASE-002-Human-AI-Delegation-and-Scoring-Sovereignty.md`, near the separation of evaluation, approval, and execution.

**Caption:** *Figure 5. Structural Freedom and Choice Generation. Candidate actions are filtered by feasibility, information quality, commitments, and authorization before accountable selection and execution; reflective review may propose—but does not automatically authorize—structural revisions.*

**Design checks:** More options do not necessarily mean more freedom. The ability to compute a recommendation does not imply permission to execute it.

### Fig-006 — Self-Referential Scoring and Metacognition

**Planned file:** `figures/Fig-006-Self-Referential-Scoring-and-Metacognition.png`  
**Primary placement:** `docs/SSI-006-Self-Referential-Scoring-Self-Models-and-Metacognition.md`, after **Section 6, “Reflective Evaluation Graph (REG)”**, or within **Section 10, “Integration with HIG, SWM, SRVC, and CPG.”**  
**Secondary placement:** `docs/SSI-008-Consciousness-Boundaries-and-the-Future-of-SSI.md`, near **Section 9, “The Self-Reference and Goodhart Boundary.”**

**Caption:** *Figure 6. Self-Referential Scoring and Metacognition. Self-model claims are checked against evidence; evaluator dependencies and counter-evidence are audited; proposed revisions pass through independent validation and an authorization gate.*

**Design checks:** Depict safeguards against circular self-certification. A self-model does not establish phenomenal consciousness, and a successful self-test does not authorize unrestricted self-modification.

### Fig-007 — Aspen Grove of Worldview Evolution

**Planned file:** `figures/Fig-007-Aspen-Grove-of-Worldview-Evolution.png`  
**Primary placement:** `docs/SSI-007-Recursive-Principle-Formation-and-Worldview-Evolution.md`, near **Section 5, “Principle Evolution Graph (PEG)”**, or **Section 9, “Historical Identity and Continuity Through Worldview Change.”**  
**Secondary placement:** `cases/CASE-003-Worldview-Evolution-and-Identity-Preservation.md`, near the case's account of principle revision and identity preservation.

**Caption:** *Figure 7. Aspen Grove of Worldview Evolution. Shared historical roots support differentiated branches of knowledge, principles, and worldview; growth includes divergence, revision, and renewal while retaining traceable lineage. The grove is a metaphor, not a claim that subjects literally share one identity.*

**Design checks:** Avoid presenting branching as automatic progress or shared knowledge as shared subject identity. Distinguish continuity from immutability.
