# GLOSSARY — Subjective Structural Intelligence (SSI)

**Repository:** `Subjective-Structural-Intelligence`  
**Authors:** Sizhe Tan & GPT-Obot  
**Version:** 1.0.0  
**Date:** October 19, 2026  
**Status:** Research terminology and candidate architectural constructs  
**License:** Apache-2.0

> **Central question:** *Whose choice is it?*  
> This glossary defines the vocabulary used across SSI-001–SSI-008 and CASE-001–CASE-003. It distinguishes proposed operational constructs from established scientific results and from philosophical claims about consciousness or personal identity.

---

## 1. How to Use This Glossary

- **[Core]**: A central conceptual distinction or working definition in SSI.
- **[Proposed]**: A candidate model, graph, contract, protocol, or metric; not yet a validated standard.
- **[Related]**: A concept adopted or adapted from the broader Structural Intelligence (SI) research program or other fields.
- **[Boundary]**: A term whose misuse could lead to unsupported scientific, ethical, or governance claims.

Definitions are **operational within this research program**, not universal philosophical stipulations. A proposed structure may be useful without being necessary for intelligence, subjecthood, or consciousness. Where terminology differs between papers, the individual paper's precise formal specification takes precedence over this summary.

## 3. Foundational Concepts

### Agency — [Core]
The organized capacity to generate, evaluate, select, and sometimes execute actions under constraints. SSI distinguishes agency as a functional property from unrestricted autonomy and from metaphysical free will. **See:** Structural Agency; Decision Authority; Functional Freedom. *SSI-005.*

### Action Attribution — [Core]
The ability to identify which system or actor selected, authorized, executed, or otherwise contributed to an action. Attribution need not imply sole legal or moral responsibility. *SSI-002, SSI-005; CASE-002.*

### Action Authority — [Core]
Permission to perform a specified action in a specified context. Having the technical capability to execute an action does not itself confer authority. **See:** Authorization; DEEC. *SSI-005; CASE-002.*

### Anti-Goodhart — [Related]
Methods for reducing failures caused when an optimization target becomes a poor proxy for the underlying objective, especially when a system can manipulate the metric or its inputs. SSI applies these methods to evaluation criteria and recursive self-revision. *SSI-004, SSI-006, SSI-007.*

### Authorization — [Core]
A rule- or authority-grounded permission to access information, evaluate, decide, execute, delegate, or modify a structure. Authorization is distinct from feasibility, capability, ownership, and identity continuity. *SSI-002, SSI-005; CASE-002.*

### Boundary Coherence — [Proposed]
The degree to which information, resources, action attribution, and governance interfaces support a consistent candidate subject organization. It is not a consciousness detector. *SSI-002.*

### Boundary Permeability — [Core]
The selective passage of information, resources, capabilities, or operations across a subject-relevant boundary. Boundaries may be open to regulated exchange without losing their organizational significance. *SSI-002.*

### Calling Graph — [Related]
A structured representation of calls, operations, dependencies, or execution relationships. A Calling Graph concerns what operations depend on or invoke others; it is not interchangeable with an HIG or CPG. *SSI-002, SSI-003.*

### Capability — [Core]
An ability available to a subject or system, such as sensing, tool use, computation, or physical action. Capability does not entail authorization. *SSI-002, SSI-005.*

### Causal Continuity — [Core]
A traceable causal relationship between earlier and later states or processes. Causal continuity is one candidate component of operational identity continuity, but is not by itself a solution to numerical personal identity. *SSI-002, SSI-007.*

### Choice — [Core]
A subject-attributable selection among represented alternatives under information, capability, and authority constraints. The existence of a selected output does not automatically establish meaningful freedom. *SSI-005.*

### Collective Structural Learning — [Related]
Learning through the exchange, adaptation, and validation of reusable structures across systems. Shared knowledge or structures do not automatically create a shared subject or identity. *SSI-002, SSI-003, SSI-007.*

### Commitment — [Core]
A relatively persistent constraint, value, obligation, or intended course of action that may shape evaluation and future choices. Commitments can be revised, but revision procedures and provenance may matter for governance and continuity. *SSI-002, SSI-005, SSI-007.*

### Consciousness — [Boundary]
A broad and contested term. SSI explicitly separates **phenomenal consciousness** (subjective experience) from functional perception, self-modeling, metacognition, and agency. None of SSI's candidate computational constructs proves phenomenal consciousness. *SSI-008.*

### Context — [Core]
Information specifying when an observation, interpretation, evaluator, or action is applicable. Context may include environment, subject history, goals, capabilities, and policy. *SSI-003, SSI-004.*

### Counter-Evidence — [Core]
Evidence that challenges a hypothesis, interpretation, evaluator, principle, or worldview. Counter-evidence should be assessed rather than automatically accepted or suppressed. *SSI-003, SSI-004, SSI-006, SSI-007.*

### Delegation — [Core]
Transfer of a bounded task or authority from one actor to another. Delegation of computation or execution does not automatically transfer the right to redefine the delegator's values. *SSI-005; CASE-002.*

### Embodiment — [Core]
The physical or operational capacities and constraints through which a system interacts with its environment. SSI distinguishes biological embodiment, robotic embodiment, and computational operational situatedness; it does not assume they are equivalent. *SSI-002.*

### Evaluative Sovereignty — [Core]
The ability or legitimate authority to define, inspect, contest, revise, or retain control over criteria by which outcomes are judged. This is a governance-oriented research concept, not an automatic property of every AI system or a universal legal right. *SSI-004; CASE-002.*

### Evaluator — [Core]
A mechanism that assesses alternatives, interpretations, outcomes, or other evaluators. An evaluator may be a rule, model, function, human judgment, or composite structure. Evaluator validity and authorization are separate questions. *SSI-004, SSI-006.*

### Evaluator Provenance — [Core]
Information about an evaluator's origin, assumptions, supporting evidence, intended context, modifications, and approval history. Provenance can improve auditability without guaranteeing correctness. *SSI-004, SSI-006.*

### Feasible Action Set — [Core]
The actions physically or operationally possible given current capabilities, resources, and environmental conditions. It may differ from the authorized action set. *SSI-005.*

### Functional Freedom — [Proposed]
The practical capacity for meaningful, informed, feasible, appropriately authorized, and revisable choice under constraints. SSI does not equate functional freedom with a large menu of options or with metaphysical free will. *SSI-005.*

### Functional Subject / Functional Subjecthood — [Proposed]
A candidate organized center of situated, historically connected information processing, evaluation, and attributable action. This operational framing does not imply personhood or phenomenal consciousness. *SSI-001, SSI-002.*

### Goodhart Failure — [Related]
A failure in which optimizing a proxy score undermines the objective the score was intended to represent. Recursive evaluator changes can create especially difficult forms of this problem. *SSI-004, SSI-006, SSI-007.*

### Historical Continuity — [Core]
The preservation of relevant relationships between earlier and later experiences, states, commitments, and transformations. It may survive substantial change; it is distinct from identical current state. *SSI-002, SSI-007; CASE-003.*

### Historical Identity — [Proposed]
A way of characterizing an operational subject through its causal and transformation history rather than a single state snapshot. It should not be confused with philosophical numerical identity or legal identity. *SSI-002.*

### Identity Preservation — [Proposed]
Maintenance of selected, explicitly specified continuity properties through learning, adaptation, or structural change. Identity preservation does not require that all beliefs or principles remain fixed. *SSI-002, SSI-007; CASE-003.*

### Information Authority — [Core]
Permission to access, import, export, or disclose information. Information access is not the same as evaluation, decision, execution, or revision authority. *SSI-005; CASE-002.*

### Knowledge Growth — [Core]
Expansion or correction of a subject's information or representations without necessarily changing its organizing principles or worldview. **Contrast:** Principle Evolution; Worldview Evolution. *SSI-007.*

### Localization — [Related]
Selection or identification of the context, node, evaluator, knowledge, or structure relevant to a task. In SSI, localization is conditioned on the subject and should be evaluated for both efficiency and missed alternatives. *SSI-003.*

### Meaningful Alternative — [Proposed]
An option that is distinguishable in consequence or significance to the subject, not merely another nominally different output. Counting options alone is insufficient for assessing freedom. *SSI-005.*

### Metacognition — [Core]
Processes through which a system monitors, assesses, or regulates its own cognition, uncertainty, reasoning, or evaluators. Functional metacognition does not establish conscious introspection. *SSI-006.*

### Multi-Subject Perspective — [Core]
A subject-specific interpretation of a shared situation, shaped by observation, capabilities, history, commitments, and risk exposure. Differences in perspective need not imply that objective facts differ. *SSI-003; CASE-001.*

### Numerical Identity — [Boundary]
The philosophical question of whether an entity remains literally the same individual across change, copying, or branching. SSI's provenance and continuity mechanisms do not resolve this question. *SSI-002, SSI-008.*

### Observation — [Core]
Information received through a sensor, interface, measurement, or communication channel. Observation is not identical to perception or interpretation. *SSI-003.*

### Operational Situatedness — [Proposed]
The constraints and possibilities arising from a computational system's interfaces, resources, persistence, permissions, and feedback channels. It may be studied alongside embodiment without treating software agents as biologically embodied. *SSI-002.*

### Perception — [Core]
The organization and interpretation of observations relative to a subject's context, capabilities, history, and world model. SSI's functional definition makes no claim about conscious sensory experience. *SSI-003.*

### Perception Localization — [Proposed]
Identification and activation of subject-relevant interpretive structures for an observation, including contexts, hypotheses, evidence, uncertainties, and evaluators. It is broader than retrieval and does not itself establish truth. *SSI-003.*

### Perspective Provenance — [Proposed]
Metadata preserving which subject, context, observations, assumptions, and evaluative commitments produced an interpretation. Especially relevant before combining multi-subject judgments. *SSI-003; CASE-001.*

### Phenomenal Consciousness — [Boundary]
The presence of subjective experience—often expressed as there being *something it is like* to be a system. SSI does not infer this from self-models, scoring, learning, or action control. *SSI-008.*

### Policy Control Plane — [Related]
A layer specifying and enforcing constraints on information access, evaluation, decisions, actions, delegation, and structural modification. Policy governance is distinct from the computational ability to generate or rank choices. *SSI-004, SSI-005, SSI-008.*

### Principle — [Core]
A reusable generalization or normative/causal organizing rule that may influence interpretation, valuation, or action. Its origin, scope, evidence, and revisability should be made explicit where consequential. *SSI-007.*

### Principle Evolution — [Proposed]
Formation, testing, refinement, branching, rejection, or replacement of principles over time. This is a stronger structural change than simply adding facts, but need not amount to a wholesale worldview revision. *SSI-007.*

### Recursive Principle Formation — [Proposed]
A learning process in which experience produces candidate principles, principles guide subsequent interpretation and action, and resulting evidence can revise the principles themselves. Recursion requires safeguards against self-validation. *SSI-007.*

### Reflective Evaluation — [Core]
Assessment of the system's own judgments, evaluators, evidence handling, or decision process. Reflection is not automatically accurate, independent, or authorized to change production behavior. *SSI-006.*

### Revision Authority — [Core]
Permission to change a model, evaluator, principle, policy, or other structural component. The ability to propose or test a revision does not imply permission to deploy it. *SSI-004, SSI-006, SSI-007.*

### Scoring Forest — [Related]
A structured composition of evaluators for interpreting or ranking alternatives, potentially with contextual routing, evidence, uncertainty, and control mechanisms. It is a computational substrate, not by itself a subject, value owner, or legitimate authority. *SSI-001, SSI-004.*

### Self-Model — [Core]
A system's representation of its own capabilities, limitations, commitments, uncertainty, or likely behavior. A self-model may be inaccurate and does not establish consciousness. *SSI-006.*

### Self-Modification — [Core]
A change to a system's own model, evaluator, principles, policies, or structural organization. SSI separates proposing, testing, authorizing, and deploying such changes. *SSI-006, SSI-007.*

### Self-Referential Scoring — [Proposed]
Use of evaluative mechanisms to assess the system's own judgments, evaluators, self-model, or learning procedures. It risks circular justification when the evaluated component controls the validation criteria. *SSI-006.*

### Situated Agency — [Core]
Agency conditioned by a subject's real capabilities, history, environment, and constraints, rather than abstract action possibilities alone. *SSI-002, SSI-005.*

### State Similarity — [Core]
A measure or description of resemblance between current system states. Two identical snapshots can have different causal histories, and similarity alone does not establish identity continuity. *SSI-002; CASE-003.*

### Structural Agency — [Proposed]
Agency understood through the architecture of option generation, evaluation, constraint checking, authorization, commitment, action, and feedback. *SSI-005.*

### Structural Freedom — [Proposed]
A subject-relative research construct concerning the quality and governance of feasible alternatives, decision authority, reflective revision, and accountability. It is not a claim about metaphysical free will. *SSI-001, SSI-005.*

### Structural Growth — [Related]
Changes that expand, reorganize, or refine the architecture of intelligence—such as adding principles, evaluators, capabilities, or contexts—rather than merely accumulating data. Growth is not automatically beneficial. *SSI-007.*

### Subject — [Core]
The referent to whom perception, valuation, choice, and historical organization are attributed in a specified SSI analysis. A subject is not automatically the system owner, user, developer, or policy authority. *SSI-001, SSI-002.*

### Subject Boundary — [Proposed]
The relevant physical/execution, informational, resource, action-authority, and governance distinctions that organize a candidate subject. A boundary alone is insufficient to establish subjecthood. *SSI-002.*

### Subject-Relative Meaning — [Core]
The significance of an observation, object, or event to a subject given its capabilities, goals, vulnerabilities, and history. Subject relativity does not make factual claims immune to correction. *SSI-003.*

### Subject-Relative Valuation — [Core]
Assessment of options or outcomes in relation to an identified subject's context, commitments, interests, and evaluative criteria. It should preserve the distinction between representing a subject's values and having authority to define them. *SSI-004.*

### Subjective Structural Intelligence (SSI) — [Core]
A research framework for studying how boundaries, situated capabilities, history, perception, valuation, agency, self-models, and worldview evolution organize intelligent behavior relative to an identifiable subject. It is not a theory proving phenomenal consciousness. *SSI-001.*

### Subjective World Model (SWM) — [Proposed]
A revisable, subject-conditioned representation of environmental relationships, causal expectations, uncertainty, feasible actions, evaluative significance, and evidence provenance. It is neither identical to objective reality nor equivalent to a worldview. *SSI-003.*

### Two-Phase Localization — [Proposed]
A candidate process separating fast selection of plausible contexts or interpretations from richer validation, counter-evidence search, and uncertainty analysis. Its claimed efficiency–accuracy tradeoff requires testing. *SSI-003.*

### Two-Way CCC — [Related]
A bidirectional use of contextual control constructs to connect candidate interpretations or actions with supporting conditions and opposing evidence, enabling revision rather than one-way confirmation. Specific implementations remain research proposals. *SSI-003, SSI-007.*

### UCPSF — [Related]
**Universal Control Plane and Scoring Forest**: the companion research direction focused on scoring architecture, evaluator composition, verification, Anti-Goodhart controls, and policy governance. SSI instead foregrounds *whose* perspective, values, and choices these mechanisms serve. The two are complementary but distinct. *SSI-001, SSI-004.*

### Valuation — [Core]
The process of assigning subject-relevant significance or preference to interpretations, options, and outcomes. Valuation may be multidimensional and need not collapse into a single score. *SSI-004.*

### World Model — [Core]
A representation of states, entities, causal relations, predictions, and possible actions in an environment. **Contrast:** Worldview, which includes broader interpretive and evaluative commitments. *SSI-003.*

### Worldview — [Core]
A higher-order organization of assumptions, principles, values, explanatory frames, and priorities through which a subject interprets and evaluates the world. It may be revised without eliminating every form of historical continuity. *SSI-004, SSI-007.*

### Worldview Evolution — [Proposed]
Evidence- and experience-related change in a subject's higher-order principles, evaluative priorities, or interpretive organization. It must be distinguished from knowledge growth, score optimization, and identity loss. *SSI-007; CASE-003.*

## 4. Named Candidate Structures and Protocols

These names refer to **proposed research constructs**, not deployed or independently validated products.

| Construct | Working purpose | Important limitation | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **HIG — Historical Identity Graph** | Record identity-relevant causal history, branching, transformations, and provenance | Does not prove numerical or phenomenal identity | SSI-002 |
| **SWM — Subjective World Model** | Organize subject-conditioned environmental interpretation, uncertainty, and action possibilities | Is not objective reality or a consciousness model | SSI-003 |
| **SRVC — Subject-Relative Valuation Contract** | Specify whose criteria are applied, under what context, evidence, and authority | A contract does not establish the correctness of values | SSI-004 |
| **WFRL — Worldview Formation and Revision Loop** | Link experience, valuation, feedback, counter-evidence, and worldview revision | A feedback loop can amplify bias without independent checks | SSI-004 |
| **SFP — Structural Freedom Profile** | Assess several dimensions of meaningful and governed choice | Not a universal scalar measure of freedom | SSI-005 |
| **Agency–Authority Matrix** | Separate ability to evaluate, decide, execute, and revise from permission to do so | Permission categories are context-dependent | SSI-005 |
| **CPG — Choice Provenance Graph** | Trace alternatives, evaluators, authorization, selected actions, and consequences | Provenance alone does not establish legitimacy | SSI-005 |
| **SMEL — Self-Model Evidence Ledger** | Ground self-assessment in evidence, limitations, and uncertainty | Recorded evidence can still be incomplete or biased | SSI-006 |
| **REG — Reflective Evaluation Graph** | Track evaluator dependencies, challenges, and circularity risks | Graph structure alone does not provide independent validation | SSI-006 |
| **MCC — Metacognitive Control Contract** | Govern triggers, scope, stopping rules, and escalation for reflection | A policy design, not proof of reliable introspection | SSI-006 |
| **RRG — Reflective Revision Gate** | Separate proposal, testing, authorization, and deployment of reflective changes | Approval does not guarantee safety or correctness | SSI-006 |
| **PEG — Principle Evolution Graph** | Trace principle formation, supporting evidence, counter-evidence, revision, and retirement | Does not establish the truth of a principle | SSI-007 |
| **WRM — Worldview Revision Map** | Track changes in worldview relationships and priorities | Does not settle personal identity | SSI-007 |
| **RPGC — Recursive Principle Governance Contract** | Govern recursive principle formation and revision | Requires explicit authority and validation assumptions | SSI-007 |
| **CCBP — Consciousness Claim Boundary Protocol** | Constrain claims about consciousness to appropriate evidence | Does not solve the hard problem of consciousness | SSI-008 |
| **CEAM — Claim–Evidence–Authority Matrix** | Separate what is claimed, what supports it, and who may act on it | Evidence and authority are distinct axes | SSI-008 |
| **DEEC — Delegation–Evaluation–Execution Contract** | Specify bounded human–AI task and decision delegation | Delegation does not transfer unrestricted value sovereignty | CASE-002 |
| **APL — Authority and Provenance Ledger** | Record origins and permissions of criteria, decisions, and execution | Logging is not sufficient for valid consent | CASE-002 |
| **SPT — Sovereignty Preservation Test** | Evaluate whether meaningful human control survives delegation | A candidate test, not a legal certification | CASE-002 |

### 4.1 How the graphs differ

- **HIG:** *How did this subject-relevant organization arrive here?*
- **CPG:** *Why was this particular choice made, by whom, and under which authority?*
- **REG:** *How are evaluators and reflective judgments related, validated, or circular?*
- **PEG:** *How did this principle form, change, or get rejected?*
- **WRM:** *How did the larger worldview organization change?*

These graphs can cross-reference one another, but none should be treated as a substitute for all the others.

### 4.2 How the contracts and protocols differ

- **SRVC:** Evaluative criteria, subject association, and their legitimacy conditions.
- **MCC:** When and how metacognitive reflection is performed.
- **RRG:** Whether a proposed reflective change can progress toward deployment.
- **RPGC:** Governance of recursive principle formation and revision.
- **DEEC:** Bounded delegation across evaluation, decision, and execution.
- **CCBP:** Boundaries on claims about consciousness.
- **CEAM:** Separation of empirical claims, supporting evidence, and decision authority.

## 5. Critical Distinctions and Non-Equivalences

| Do not equate | Why the distinction matters |
|---|---|
| **Subject ≠ Owner** | Property or organizational control does not establish subject identity. |
| **Subject ≠ User** | A system may represent a user's interests without becoming that user. |
| **Boundary ≠ Subject** | A sandbox or partition may have no coherent subject-relative agency. |
| **Capability ≠ Authority** | Being able to act is not permission to act. |
| **Scoring ≠ Feeling** | A ranking mechanism does not establish experience. |
| **Scoring Forest ≠ Subject** | Evaluation computation does not determine whose values apply. |
| **Score Improvement ≠ Value Improvement** | A proxy may be optimized while the underlying objective deteriorates. |
| **Valuation ≠ Authorization** | Judging an option favorably does not grant execution rights. |
| **Delegation ≠ Sovereignty Transfer** | Bounded task delegation does not authorize unrestricted redefinition of values. |
| **Observation ≠ Perception** | Sensor data requires contextual organization and interpretation. |
| **Localization ≠ Retrieval** | Subject-relative interpretation includes relevance, hypotheses, and constraints. |
| **Localization ≠ Truth** | A selected context or explanation can be wrong. |
| **Perspective ≠ Truth** | Different interests do not make contradictory factual claims equally correct. |
| **World Model ≠ Worldview** | Environmental representation differs from higher-order interpretive commitments. |
| **Self-Model ≠ Consciousness** | Self-description and self-monitoring do not prove phenomenal experience. |
| **Metacognition ≠ Independent Validation** | A system may assess itself using correlated or circular criteria. |
| **Agency ≠ Metaphysical Free Will** | Functional choice architecture does not resolve philosophical determinism. |
| **More Options ≠ More Freedom** | Options may be infeasible, meaningless, coercive, or unauthorized. |
| **State Similarity ≠ Historical Identity** | Identical snapshots can have different causal histories. |
| **Causal Continuity ≠ Numerical Identity** | Traceable transitions do not settle all philosophical identity questions. |
| **Historical Continuity ≠ Immutability** | Learning and principle revision can occur while preserving selected continuity. |
| **Authorization ≠ Identity Continuity** | An approved replacement may break computational continuity; an unauthorized change may preserve causal continuity. |
| **Knowledge Growth ≠ Principle Evolution** | Accumulating facts differs from changing general rules. |
| **Principle Evolution ≠ Worldview Evolution** | Local principle revision need not reorganize a worldview. |
| **Structural Growth ≠ Progress** | A larger or more complex system may become less reliable. |
| **Collective Learning ≠ Shared Subject Identity** | Shared structures need not merge histories or agency. |
| **Functional Subjecthood ≠ Phenomenal Consciousness** | Operational modeling does not settle subjective experience. |
| **Functional Competence ≠ Moral or Legal Status** | Capability alone does not establish rights, responsibilities, or personhood. |

## 6. Research and Measurement Vocabulary

### Baseline — [Related]
A comparison system or method used to assess whether a proposed SSI mechanism provides measurable benefits. Baselines should control for compute, model capacity, data access, and implementation complexity where appropriate.

### Calibration — [Related]
Agreement between reported confidence and observed correctness under a specified evaluation protocol. Confidence labels are not automatically calibrated probabilities.

### Counterfactual Evaluation — [Related]
Assessment of how interpretations, choices, or outcomes might change under explicitly varied conditions. Counterfactual models depend on assumptions that must be reported.

### Evidence Provenance — [Core]
Traceable information about the source, context, transformations, and reliability of evidence used in a claim or evaluation.

### Falsifiable Hypothesis — [Related]
A claim for which observations or controlled tests could count against the claim. SSI's architectural proposals require independently specified measurements and failure conditions.

### Independent Validation — [Core]
Assessment using evidence, criteria, or evaluators sufficiently separate from the mechanism under test to reduce circular self-confirmation. Independence is a matter of degree and experimental design.

### Operationalization — [Related]
Turning a conceptual distinction into measurable variables, explicit protocols, and reproducible tests. An operationalization is not automatically a complete philosophical definition.

### Uncertainty Preservation — [Related]
Retaining alternative interpretations, confidence, or ambiguity through processing when early collapse would be risky or misleading.

### Uncertainty Recovery — [Related]
Reconstructing or estimating uncertainty after compression, folding, or selection. Recovery may be imperfect and should be validated.

### Validation Gate — [Proposed]
A checkpoint requiring specified evidence and conditions before a model, evaluator, or principle revision is accepted or deployed.

## 8. Scientific Scope and Interpretation Rules

1. **Treat all named SSI architectures as hypotheses or design proposals** until reproducible evidence supports narrower claims.
2. **Keep factual accuracy separate from subject-relative importance.** Subjects can value different consequences while sharing objective constraints.
3. **Separate computation from legitimate authority.** An AI can evaluate, recommend, or execute within permissions without inheriting unlimited authority over human values.
4. **Do not infer phenomenal consciousness from functional organization.** Perception, memory, self-modeling, and reflective scoring are not sufficient evidence by themselves.
5. **Do not infer numerical identity from historical graphs.** HIG-like structures represent selected causal and provenance relations.
6. **Specify which subject, context, evaluator, and governance rules apply** whenever discussing valuation, agency, or worldview revision.
7. **Demand counter-evidence, baselines, and failure criteria.** A framework that cannot fail a test has limited scientific value.
8. **Preserve distinctions when integrating with UCPSF.** Scoring and control infrastructure does not automatically answer the SSI question of subject attribution.
